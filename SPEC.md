# Bobert specification

Status: Bobert 1.0.0 is the first release under the name Bobert (formerly Cyclegauge, whose last update is 0.8.0 with a move notice); it is not yet published. It includes the user-triggered workspace audit. Implemented: log collector, IDE extension, account history and qualified forecasts, independent cost engine behind the session-only Cost Advisor, pooled history forecasting and the usage trajectory chart. Sections below labeled by milestone are design records unless stated otherwise. The VSIX was installed and its status bar and details verified in macOS Kiro 1.2.56. Discovery evidence is documented in [data findings](docs/kiro-data-format.md).

## Purpose

Provide local credit tracking and qualified forecasts that distinguish account-wide consumption from the portion attributable to retained local sessions. Support a future cross-platform application for macOS, Windows, Ubuntu, and RHEL without requiring Kiro credentials or changing Kiro state.

## Data authority

1. **Account snapshots are the source of truth for current observed account usage.** Use the freshest valid, comparable account snapshot from the service-response logs or Kiro's cached usage. Display its as-of time, source, and freshness; a stale snapshot is not a live balance or final invoice.
2. **Session metering provides observed local daily attribution.** It may omit other devices, cloud work, deleted sessions, active turns, and unsupported formats. Never substitute this sum for account usage, remaining credits, or plan capacity.
3. Compare sources for reconciliation; never add an account snapshot or snapshot delta to session totals. Report differences as unexplained coverage, timing, account-association, or adjustment differences, not automatically missing billable work.
4. CLI `metering_usage[]` semantics are unresolved. Verify additive versus cumulative behavior for the applicable format/version before enabling its aggregation. Until then, report metering as present but uninterpreted, with no credit total derived from those entries.

## Required behavior and implementation scope

- Select the `CREDIT` resource and prefer `*WithPrecision` fields. Preserve decimal values without rounding until presentation. Do not confuse `INVOCATIONS`, token counts, or context percentages with billing units.
- Attribute accepted local turn summaries to their completion day in the configured IANA timezone; default to the device timezone, initially Europe/Berlin. Label this as completion-time attribution, not exact within-turn consumption timing.
- Keep billing boundaries in UTC. Prefer explicit reset timestamps over a computed calendar assumption.
- Persist observations in Bobert-owned storage so future coverage can improve. Track observation time separately from ingestion time; re-reading an old cache must not make it fresh.
- Treat positive same-cycle snapshot differences as observed **net counter changes** over an interval. Daily consumption within an interval spanning days remains unknown. Do not interpolate by default or fill uncovered days with zero.
- Treat advancing reset dates as cycle-change evidence. A counter decrease alone is an anomaly, not proof of reset. Preserve adjustments, plan/account changes, and reset ambiguity.
- Keep plans, base credits, overages, bonuses, and add-on credits distinct. Unknown bonus schemas must not be folded into a fabricated remaining-credit balance.
- Preserve provenance, partial coverage, unsupported-format status, stale/conflicting observations, and unresolved account association. A missing value is not zero.
- Milestone 3 forecasting discloses its method, observation window, policy and coverage. Insufficient coverage yields unavailable estimates; interval net rates never imply complete daily measurements.

## Session hints (advisory)

Status: implemented for 1.0.0 (unreleased until published), IDE format only, default on; enabled on macOS, Windows and Linux (maintainer decision of 2026-10-18, also for Windows and Linux; an unexpected session folder or path format leaves the feature silent; the narrow runtime read of session metadata was approved the same day, not session aggregation). From the newest IDE session of the open workspace the extension may show (a) a context-fill reminder at about 70 % and 85 % and (b) a hint when the session's model is listed above Auto's credit rate and there is a reason for it (the gauge shows a tight budget or the session is at least 30 % full; maintainer decision of 2026-10-20) in a dated in-extension table. These are advisory observations labelled "as of HH:MM": the percentage is a snapshot of the selected model's window and not Kiro's live meter, the multiplier is Kiro's published relative rate ("about Nx", actual credits vary), thresholds are educated guesses and only one table row is confirmed by a real session. Silent when the data is missing, stale, out of range, unmatched or uncertain; never stored, never added to account totals, never fed to the forecast. Details and limits: [privacy and storage](docs/privacy-and-storage.md).

## Privacy and platform requirements

Follow [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md). No credentials, raw transcript persistence, raw identifiers in diagnostics, telemetry of private data, or authenticated Kiro API calls. Billing-only normalized observations remain local and outside this repository.

macOS source locations and sample shapes were inspected. Bobert is released for macOS, Windows and Linux (maintainer decision 2026-10-22). Support native path APIs, configurable data roots, Unicode/spaces, explicit timezone handling, and safe unavailable states. Do not disable Windows protections or RHEL SELinux to gain access.

## Milestone 2 IDE extension

The primary product is a VS Code-compatible Kiro IDE extension. It imports the existing read-only IDE account-response logs on activation and when they change. A minute timer provides a fallback and reevaluates staleness even without new logs. The status bar shows remaining base credits; clicking opens a local detail view with usage, base limit, remaining base credits, reset, the 24-hour budget allocation (CLI) and today's allowance (see "Today's usage and day budget" below), snapshot time, freshness and warnings. Add-ons and overages are not folded into remaining base credits.

The extension does not invoke Kiro commands or authenticated services. It retains the Milestone 1 account isolation, decimal handling and app-owned store. It does not implement separate CLI/Crew collectors. The source timezone defaults to the device timezone unless configured; that assumption must be visible in data quality. A rescan never changes an observation's source timestamp.

The installed macOS Kiro 1.2.56 app declares Electron 44.4.3, whose documented Node version is 24.21.0. The VSIX was installed and activated inside this IDE; its live account reading confirms the extension host can run the bundled collector and `node:sqlite`. The exact extension-host `process.versions.node` value was not separately captured. Windows, Ubuntu and RHEL remain unverified targets.

## Current acceptance boundary

Milestone 1 implements the log-only account collector, local SQLite persistence and CLI described in [docs/usage.md](docs/usage.md). Milestone 2 adds the IDE extension and local UI. Milestone 3 adds account history and qualified forecasts. Session attribution, daemon and Kiro SQLite access remain deferred. TypeScript is bundled to JavaScript for VSIX; runtime has no external dependencies.

The CLI base-credit allocation divides observed remaining base credits by remaining UTC 24-hour days until explicit reset. It is a budget allocation, not statistical forecasting or known daily consumption. Its freshness threshold defaults to 24 hours and is configurable. Multiple/unknown account partitions and equal-time billing conflicts withhold the selected account view. Unsupported bonuses do not establish total availability. Windows default storage relies on the user profile's permissions; a chosen storage location uses owner-only NTFS DACL enforcement and fails closed if it cannot be verified; Windows, Ubuntu and RHEL remain unverified.

## Milestone 3: history, budget and forecasts

`history()` and `forecast()` are pure billing-only core modules. Existing owned persistence/account isolation remains unchanged. Group equal-time observations by instant, retain conflicts and select the latest contiguous explicit-reset cycle. History exposes points, classified net-change intervals and UTC days. A day receives only intervals contained wholly within it (including an endpoint at next midnight); it is complete only when comparable intervals cover all 24 hours. Missing/cross-day periods remain unknown. A partial daily sum is observed net change for the supported part of that day, not its total consumption.

`averageObservedNetChangePerDay` is the comparable window's net counter change divided by elapsed UTC 24-hour days. It may describe a gap-spanning interval with known endpoints, but is explicitly not a complete daily series or an eligible forecast by itself. Counter decreases, changed limits, conflicts, missing fields and unsupported extras suspend comparisons. Daily history/coverage is limited to 3,660 elapsed days; excess ranges fail unavailable as an operational bound.

Forecast schema/policy version 2 exposes `tier`: `provisional`, `regular` or `unavailable`, for the result and each method. Provisional estimates require at least three distinct comparable account snapshots spanning at least 48 elapsed hours. Regular forecasts preserve at least eight distinct comparable snapshots spanning seven days and automatically take precedence. Both require no interval exceeding 36 hours, unchanged known account/limit/reset, nonnegative comparable changes and a latest observation at most 24 hours old by default. Only `observed_window` supports the provisional tier; rolling and cycle-to-date retain their regular gates. These are conservative operational thresholds, not empirical accuracy guarantees. Zero observed net rate is valid; missing observations never become zeros. Historical barriers exclude every window crossing them; after a clean 48-hour/three-snapshot window, provisional window forecasts can resume; after seven days/eight snapshots, regular window forecasts can resume while cycle-to-date remains suspended.

At-least average since the cycle start (`cycleAverageFloor`, schema version 4; a lower bound, never an estimate). Kiro's public documentation says that plan credits renew at the start of the next billing cycle but does not document when a cycle starts, so a start is never derived from the reset date. Under the *assumption* that a cycle lasts at most 31 days (`maximum_cycle_length_assumed`; an assumption, not an observation), `forecast()` reports `{ ratePerDay, from, to, basis, projectedUsageAtResetFloor, budgetComparison }` where `from` is the evidence-backed cycle start (`basis: evidence_cycle_start`) or else the reset minus 31 days (`basis: maximum_cycle_length`), `to` is the latest observation, `ratePerDay` is latest usage divided by that time, and the projection and budget comparison use the same exact formulas as the estimates. Because the real start is never earlier than `from`, the real average since the cycle start is at least this rate. The object is `null` whenever `forecast()` stops before the window checks (stale, reset passed, missing billing, unsupported extra credits, …), when the elapsed time is not positive, and when the first update of the current cycle is earlier than reset minus 31 days (`cycle_longer_than_assumed`, the assumption is contradicted). It is not in `estimates`, is never `selectedMethod` and never changes `tier` or `status`; the forecast policy stays version 3. It is also withheld when the latest update has reduced source precision (an integer counter may be rounded up).

Allowance display while collecting. When no forecast is eligible, the latest update is fresh and the daily budget is known, the presentation shows credits left and the daily allowance instead of a bare "Collecting data": the status bar reads `<remaining> left • <used>/<allowance> today` (or `<remaining> left • <allowance>/day` without a comparable update today, or `<remaining> left • day off` on a day that is not a budget day; truncated to one decimal like every compact figure; see the day budget below), the heading `Up to <allowance> credits/day until the reset`, and the explanation names credits left, days to the reset and the allowance, then, when `cycleAverageFloor` exists and is positive, "So far this cycle you have used at least … credits per day on average" (with the assumption clause for `maximum_cycle_length`). A floor above the allowance adds "Even this minimum is above your daily allowance." (when budget days are restricted: "…above the <budget> credits per calendar day that would last until the reset."), the warning tone in the detail view and the warning icon and background in the status bar (severity warning), never "Likely to exceed", an exhaustion date or "On track". The tier line still reads "Forecast not ready yet". This is presentation only; a stale or unavailable state is unchanged. With no base credits left the previous collecting text stays. The floor sentence needs a floor of at least 0.1; the warning (status bar and hero) needs a floor spanning at least one day whose displayed value is above the displayed allowance.

Today's usage and day budget (extension presentation only; maintainer decision of 2026-10-28, see [the proposal](docs/daily-budget-days-proposal.md)). The status bar and the credit view show today's observed usage against a day-anchored allowance, for example `On track • 55/100 today`. The calculation is a pure module that uses only the account history the extension already holds.
- **Day.** Today is the local calendar day of the device time zone, not the UTC day; a day can be 23 or 25 hours long at a daylight-saving change.
- **Used today** is the usage at the latest update minus the usage at the first update of the local day. It is an observed lower bound: usage before that first update is unknown, not zero, and the tooltip says from when it is measured. It is shown only when every update of today and every interval between them is comparable (same reset and limit, no conflicts, no missing values, no unsupported extra credits).
- **Day-anchored allowance.** The anchor is the first comparable update of today, or the latest update if there is none yet; it must be fresh. Today's allowance is the remaining base credits at the anchor divided by the sum of the budget-day weights from today to the day that contains the reset, so it stays fixed during the day. Without an update today the status bar shows the allowance from the latest update as `<allowance>/day`.
- **Budget days** (`bobert.budgetDays`, default all seven weekdays). A day that is not a budget day has weight 0. A budget day has weight 1; the exception is a later reset day, which counts the share of that day before the reset. Today always has weight 1, even when the reset falls later today, so the allowance never exceeds the credits left. If today is not a budget day, the result is a day off (`On track • day off`, or `• 12 today (day off)` with usage); usage on a day off lowers the remaining credits and so the next budget day's allowance. If no budget day is left before the reset, no allowance is shown. The setting does not change the forecast, which still averages over observed calendar time.
- **Unchanged.** `availablePerDay` (24-hour days until the reset), the CLI, the forecast, the Store and risk colours are unchanged; risk colour and notices stay tied to the projection, and exceeding today's allowance raises no warning. Stale or unavailable data shows no day figures.
- **Setup and notice.** `Bobert: Set Up` (`bobert.setup`) asks up to three quick picks (budget days, budget notices, session hints) and writes only Bobert's own user settings on the user's choice. A one-time notice ("New: spread your daily budget only over the days you work?", **Choose days** / **No thanks**) is shown only in Kiro, once, to users who used Bobert before this version. Prompt priority: budget notice, then support prompt, then this notice. They never show at the same time; a budget notice that comes due while the budget-days notice is open waits until it closes, and a held-back budget-days notice can show in a later window. An unanswered budget-days notice can therefore hold back a budget notice until it is answered or the window closes (accepted). The configuration-review offer and session hints are not part of this ordering.

Methods: `observed_window` uses endpoint delta / elapsed time; `rolling_7_day` requires exact observed boundaries seven days apart; `cycle_to_date` uses observed cycle usage / elapsed cycle time only when an advancing prior reset establishes the start, the first observation lies within 24 hours of that start, and the entire retained cycle passes coverage gates. Never compute a start by subtracting a calendar month. Rolling rate is preferred, then cycle rate, then qualified observation-window fallback. Compare consecutive exact seven-day windows for trends only if both pass the gates.

Projection is observed usage + net rate × (reset − **observation time**). Projected remaining is observed limit − projected usage; a negative value denotes base-credit shortfall. Exhaustion is conditional on the same constant rate and shown only as a UTC day before reset; zero rates do not yield an exhaustion date. Already exhausted is explicitly as-of, and exhaustion at reset is not before reset. Daily budget is max(limit − observed usage, 0) / (reset − **query time**) in UTC 24-hour days. Rate versus that allocation is a planning comparison, not measured consumption since the snapshot. That intervening usage remains unknown.

Amounts stay decimal strings; BigInt rational arithmetic avoids binary floating-point billing errors. Calculated rates/projections are truncated to six decimals for presentation; projection/exhaustion arithmetic uses original decimal delta and exact duration rather than the displayed rate. Estimates retain `qualified` eligibility status, explicit assumptions and coverage; this is not confidence. Schema v2 removes the former `confidence` field in favor of `tier`. There are no statistical probability bands. Stale, future, ambiguous/conflicting, expired-reset or insufficient data yield fixed unavailable reasons; observed as-of billing fields remain distinct from estimates. Unsupported add-on schemas cannot supply a total-availability forecast.

The extension labels the selected forecast “Provisional estimate – limited history”, “Regular forecast”, or “Forecast unavailable” with fixed reasons. It shows the selected window, elapsed days and comparable snapshot count; limited-history text explains that the window may not represent future usage. Both tiers show reset usage, remaining base credits and a conditional exhaustion day when mathematically supported. The extension status bar emphasizes daily budget and derived outlook instead of duplicating remaining credits. Stale/unavailable data cannot show a reassuring outlook; provisional tiers remain identified. Tight-budget presentation means the displayed rate is at least 90% of daily budget while not above it, without changing core forecast gates. A responsive four-card summary leads the detail view, followed by current status, forecast, observed history and quality/notes; exact values and eligibility evidence stay in expandable technical details. The extension adds a textual history/forecast summary with expandable recent observations (8), daily changes (14) and gaps (8). CLI JSON receives the same billing-only contract. No new collectors, services, dependencies or migrations in Milestone 3; the graphical chart arrived separately in 0.5.0.

## Future product direction

Evolve into a Kiro Usage & Cost Optimizer after Milestone 3. A separate configurable/versioned pricing-and-rules engine will compare current/higher/lower plans, add-on purchases and existing purchased capacity, and estimate savings over 1/3/6 months. Actual costs need independent evidence and remain separate from hypothetical scenarios and future expenditure. Never equate credits with money or infer plan/add-on balance from a displayed combined limit. Consumer rollover/expiration/proration and Enterprise overages need distinct rules.

Future efficiency insights are limited to validated non-content metadata and evidence-backed comparisons. Duration or non-spec workflows do not imply inefficiency. Content analysis requires separate explicit privacy/security design review and opt-in authorization; AGENTS safeguards remain binding.

## Milestone 4C: pooled long-term history (0.4.1)

`history()` additionally returns billing-only `evidence` for at most the trailing 85 UTC days: per-cycle intervals with exact net counter changes, UTC day rows (`netChange`, `observedMilliseconds`, `zeroNetMilliseconds`, `snapshots`) and unattributed spans. Counters are never subtracted across a reset, the time after a cycle's last snapshot is excluded rather than zero, and base-limit changes, reset regressions or unknown resets and unsupported extra credits end the evidence regime. An unchanged counter between two real snapshots is *observed zero net change*, not proof of inactivity; a positive interval longer than 36 hours remains a quality barrier for the three in-cycle methods.

Forecast schema/policy version 3 (schema version 4 since `cycleAverageFloor` was added; policy unchanged) appends `history_7_day`, `history_14_day`, `history_28_day` and `history_84_day`: exact `evidenceCredits / evidenceMilliseconds` over complete UTC days before the latest observation's day. Each requires observed exposure, snapshot count, distinct snapshot days and independence (no single interval above half the exposure). Selection is `history_28_day`, `history_14_day`, `rolling_7_day`, `history_7_day`, `cycle_to_date`, `observed_window`. A single completed run of at least five fully observed zero-net UTC days followed by observed usage is excluded from pooled exposure; sustained-change selection is implemented but disabled by default and its observations are reported as `qualifiers`. The Cost Optimizer reuses the selected exact evidence and, for future full cycles, the longest qualified pooled window.
